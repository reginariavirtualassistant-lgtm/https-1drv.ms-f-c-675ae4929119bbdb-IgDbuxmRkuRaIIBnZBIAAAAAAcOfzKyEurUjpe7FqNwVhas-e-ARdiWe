# BronzeManna concept validation — interview and pilot kit

## What is being tested
- **Family Benefits Hub:** Does one household view reduce navigation burden compared with separate benefit sites?
- **Benefits Guardian:** Can plain-language, source-linked explanations help people understand fictional notices without implying an eligibility decision?
- **Cross-program Action Planner:** Do unified reminders help people identify their next required step?
- **Agency Intelligence Portal:** Would a state or authorized processor value a controlled, measurable workflow rather than another consumer-only balance app?

## Interviews — no real case details
**Recipients (target 8–12):** Show the fictional Diana Prince flow. Ask: Which tasks are confusing? What would you trust? Which programs would you want together? What would you never want shared? Can you distinguish a sample notification from an official notice? Record reactions without collecting SSNs, EBT PINs, Medicaid IDs, diagnoses, documents or account screenshots.

**WIC staff (target 3–5):** Ask about existing vendor tools, approved product data, participant messaging, appointments, accessibility, approval authority, integration boundaries and procurement cycles.

**Medicaid administrators / MCO staff (target 3–5):** Ask about member portals, renewal workflows, coverage vs cash misconceptions, health-plan allowances, privacy requirements, messaging APIs, and approved digital member IDs.

**EBT processors (target 2–4):** Ask about read-only balance APIs, issuer permissions, fraud/security expectations, card provisioning constraints, certification, integration costs and liability.

**Procurement/security (target 2–3):** Ask what evidence is needed for pilot authorization, contracting, accessibility testing, data-processing agreements, penetration testing, SOC 2/FedRAMP applicability and state-specific compliance.

## Example interview prompts
1. Walk through how you currently complete this task.
2. Which step is hardest or most likely to be missed?
3. Does this demo make the next step clearer? Show me.
4. What information should never cross from one program to another?
5. What would make you distrust this interface?
6. Which existing system already handles this well?
7. What result would justify a pilot or purchase?
8. Who has authority to approve integration and procurement?

## Test tasks and success criteria (proposed, not validated)
- Find Diana's next Medicaid deadline in <=60 seconds.
- Find Avery's WIC action without opening a different app.
- Ask Guardian what a renewal notice means and identify its cited fictional source.
- Tell the difference between Medicaid coverage and an actual spendable plan allowance.
- Agency user filters the queue to their program and simulates a notice.
- Every participant understands that no real messages or benefits are connected.

## Scorecard
Record role, task success, completion time, confusion points, perceived trust (1–5), existing alternative, privacy concerns, and willingness to pilot. Do not store sensitive personal information.

## Pilot gates
1. Concept interviews demonstrate unmet need and an agency sponsor.
2. Written integration feasibility assessment with each agency/vendor.
3. Privacy impact assessment, threat model, consent/data minimization, accessibility review.
4. Formal procurement, security and legal approvals before any real data.
5. Only then: limited, authorized pilot with documented success metrics.

**Do not claim uniqueness, government endorsement, HIPAA compliance, real AI, or payment capability based on this demo.**
